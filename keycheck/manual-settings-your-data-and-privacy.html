Settings, your data and privacy

Every setting, your subscription, where your data is kept, backups and privacy

    This chapter is a reference. It lists every setting in KeyCheck with what it starts as and where to read more, explains managing your subscription, and sets out where your data is kept, what KeyCheck does with it and how to back it up or delete it.

    To open Settings on an iPhone or iPad, go to the Projects screen and tap Settings (the gear button). On an iPad in a wide window, the gear is at the top of the sidebar. On the Mac, choose KeyCheck → Settings (⌘,).

    A few things worth knowing:

    • Meter photos has an Experimental badge. Meter reading can misread values, so check each one against the photo.
    • Days opens a list of the days of the week. Reminders only come on the ticked days, and anything falling due on another day comes in the next ticked day's reminder. One day always stays ticked; to stop reminders altogether, turn Reminders off.
    • When you turn Reminders on, KeyCheck asks to send you notifications. If you say no, Reminders stays off. If notifications for KeyCheck are turned off in the Settings app, the note under Needs attention says so.
    • When iCloud sync is on, the iCloud sync section shows its Status, with Sync now and Turn off iCloud sync.

    Which settings follow you to other devices

    Your branding profiles and reusable templates are kept with your projects, so with iCloud sync on they appear on all your devices. Your name is the same on all your devices too when sync is on. Everything else (appearance, haptics, Needs attention and meter photos) is set on each device separately.

    Managing your subscription

    Settings → Subscription shows whether KeyCheck Pro is active. You'll see one of these:

    StatusWhat it means
    ActiveYour subscription is running.
    Cancels at period endYou've cancelled. It stays active until the current period ends.
    Switches to a planYou've changed plan, and the new one starts at the next renewal.
    Active, billing issueApple couldn't take the payment but is still trying, and you can keep working. Update your payment details in the App Store.
    Billing retryApple is still trying to take the payment. Until it goes through, projects can be opened and exported but not changed.
    ExpiredThe subscription has ended.
    RevokedThe App Store refunded or withdrew it. Contact Apple Support for billing questions.
    Not activeYou haven't subscribed on this Apple Account.

    Restore Purchases

    On a new iPhone, iPad or Mac, or after reinstalling KeyCheck, tap Restore Purchases if KeyCheck doesn't recognise your subscription straight away. Use the same Apple Account you subscribed with. The note underneath says Active subscription restored. or No active subscription was found to restore.

    Changing plan or cancelling

    Your subscription is with Apple, not with KeyCheck, so plan changes, cancelling and refunds are all handled by Apple.

    1. Tap Settings → Subscription → KeyCheck Pro.
    2. Tap Manage in App Store.

    You can also do it from your Apple Account settings. The subscription renews automatically unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the end of the current period.

    When a subscription ends

    Nothing is deleted. Your projects stay where they are and can still be opened, read and exported, including PDFs and .keycheck files. You just can't change anything, create projects, or add branding profiles or templates, and a View only notice appears at the top of each project. Subscribe again and everything can be changed as before.

    Your data

    Where it's kept

    • On your device. Your projects, plots, photos, signatures, documents, branding and templates are saved on the iPhone, iPad or Mac you're using.
    • In your iCloud, if you turn on iCloud sync. They're then also stored in your own iCloud account, so they appear on your other devices. It uses your iCloud storage. Projects you share with others are stored in your iCloud too. iCloud sync is off until you turn it on (chapter 12).

    KeyCheck's developer doesn't run any servers and never receives your project data.

    What leaves your device, and what never does

    KeyCheck never sends your projects anywhere except your own iCloud (if sync is on) and the people you choose to share a project with. There are no adverts, no analytics and no tracking.

    A few features use Apple services:

    • The weather badge sends your approximate location to Apple Weather to get the forecast. KeyCheck doesn't store your location or add it to your projects.
    • The projects map sends a project's site address and postcode to Apple Maps to find where it is. No other project details are sent.
    • The subscription is checked with Apple to see whether it's active. KeyCheck never sees your payment details.

    Anything else only leaves your device when you send it yourself: a PDF, a spreadsheet or a .keycheck file you email, AirDrop or save to Files. Where it goes after that is up to you.

    Done on your device

    These all happen on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. Nothing is uploaded to do them.

    • Reading meter photos. Readings and serial numbers are read on your device, and always shown for you to confirm. Where Apple Intelligence is available, it double-checks the reading, still on your device (chapter 5).
    • Dictating log entries. Your speech is turned into text on your device and isn't sent anywhere. Where Apple Intelligence is available, it tidies what you said into an entry for you to check, also on your device (chapter 8).
    • Reading planning documents. Apple Intelligence helps sort planning conditions on your device (chapter 7).
    • Needs attention reminders. They're worked out on your device (chapter 9).

    Backing up

    There are two ways to keep a safe copy of your projects, and it's worth using both.

    • iCloud sync (chapter 12) keeps a copy in your iCloud, so if you lose or replace a device, your projects come back when you sign in and turn sync on. But it's a copy that stays in step: if you delete a project, it's deleted everywhere.
    • KeyCheck files are a separate copy that you keep. On the Projects screen, tap ••• then Export projects…, choose the projects and tap Export. Each project is saved as its own .keycheck file, with its plots, photos, signatures and documents. Save them somewhere safe, such as Files, OneDrive or your company's shared drive. On the Mac, right-click a project in the sidebar and choose Export….

    To bring a project back from a file, open the file in KeyCheck, or go to Settings → Import → Import editable project…. Chapter 13 explains KeyCheck files in full.

    From the office

    Export your projects to .keycheck files at the end of each job, and keep them with the rest of the job's records. Then you have a copy that doesn't depend on any one device or iCloud account.

    Recently Deleted

    When you delete one of your own projects, it moves to Recently Deleted for 30 days, so a mistake can be put right. After 30 days it's deleted for good, from all your devices and for everyone it's shared with.

    • iPhone and iPad: on the Projects screen, tap ••• then Recently Deleted. On an iPad in a wide window, it's at the end of the sidebar list once something has been deleted.
    • Mac: click the Recently Deleted button (the bin) at the foot of the sidebar. It only appears when something has been deleted.

    Each project shows when it was deleted and how many days are left. Tap Restore to put it back, or Delete Now to delete it straight away. Delete Now can't be undone: its plots, photos, signatures and documents are deleted from all your devices and for everyone it's shared with.

    Restoring a project counts as a change, so it needs KeyCheck Pro.

    If someone else shared a project with you, removing it is called Leave, not Delete. It's removed from your device straight away and doesn't go to Recently Deleted. The owner's copy isn't touched.

    Deleting everything

    There's no single button to delete all your data. To remove it:

    1. Delete each project in the Projects list, then open Recently Deleted and tap Delete Now on each one. With iCloud sync on, this removes them from iCloud and your other devices too.
    2. In Settings, delete any branding profiles and reusable templates you don't want. One branding profile always stays.
    3. Delete the KeyCheck app from the device. This removes everything KeyCheck has stored on that device.

    Deleting the app on its own doesn't remove what's in iCloud. If you used iCloud sync, either delete the projects in KeyCheck first (step 1), or remove KeyCheck's data from iCloud in your device's iCloud storage settings.

    .keycheck files and PDFs you've exported are separate copies. Delete those wherever you saved them.

    If KeyCheck can't open your data

    Very rarely, KeyCheck may not be able to open its saved data when it starts, for example after a problem with the device's storage. Instead of closing, it shows Couldn't open your KeyCheck data. Your projects are still on the device. You can:

    • Try again.
    • Share data files, so they can be sent off to be recovered.
    • Start with empty data…. This moves your current data aside into a backup on the device before starting fresh. It isn't deleted.

    If this happens, try Try again first, then get in touch before starting fresh.

    If you can't change anything

    If a project shows a View only notice, or you can't edit things in Settings, it's for one of two reasons:

    • There's no KeyCheck Pro subscription on this Apple Account, or it has ended. See Managing your subscription above, and chapter 2.
    • Someone shared the project with you as view only. Ask the owner to give you permission to make changes. Chapter 12 explains sharing.

    Either way, you can still open, read and export everything.

    The privacy policy

    The full KeyCheck privacy policy explains what data the app handles, where it's kept and your rights under UK and EU data protection law. It's also linked from the bottom of the subscription screen.

    If you record details about other people in KeyCheck, such as purchasers, residents or colleagues, your company is responsible for having a good reason to keep them and for telling those people how they're used.
